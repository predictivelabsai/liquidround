# M&A Synergy Scoring Methodology

In M&A, synergies represent the additional value created when the combined entity exceeds the sum of its parts (e.g., cost savings, revenue uplift, strategic advantages). A structured scoring methodology helps evaluate potential fit objectively during due diligence, prioritize deals, and set realistic post-merger integration (PMI) targets.

A typical approach uses qualitative and quantitative dimensions, scored on a 1–5 scale (1 = weak/no fit or high risk of negative synergy; 5 = strongest fit/high confidence in material positive impact). Scores are based on data from due diligence, benchmarks, financial modeling, and expert judgment. Aggregate them into an overall weighted score.

## 1. Key Dimensions to Evaluate

Categorize dimensions into major buckets (inspired by common frameworks like functional/operational, revenue, strategic, and financial synergies). Assess each with supporting evidence (e.g., overlap analysis, interviews, benchmarks).

**Cost/Operational Synergies** (often the most reliable and quickest to realize):
- Procurement & Supply Chain: Overlap in suppliers, volume leverage, economies of scale.
- Overhead/Administrative: SG&A reductions (e.g., duplicated functions, real estate, IT systems consolidation).
- Manufacturing/Operations: Capacity utilization, process efficiencies, facility rationalization.
- R&D/Technology Integration: Shared platforms, IP leverage, reduced duplication.

**Revenue Synergies** (harder to achieve, lower realization rates ~25–35% typically):
- Cross-Selling & Customer Overlap: Complementary products/services to existing customers; expanded customer base.
- Market Expansion & Pricing Power: New geographies, channels, or segments; reduced competition.
- Product/Service Enhancement: Bundling, innovation acceleration, brand strength.

**Strategic & Business Model Synergies:**
- Market Position & Competitive Advantage: Enhanced market share, barriers to entry, or ecosystem positioning.
- Innovation & Capabilities: Complementary tech, talent, or R&D pipelines.
- Vertical/Horizontal Integration: Supply chain control or broader portfolio.

**Financial Synergies:**
- Capital Structure & Cost of Capital: Better financing terms, tax shields (e.g., NOLs), working capital optimization.
- Risk Diversification: Portfolio effects lowering volatility.

**Organizational & Cultural Fit** (critical for realization; often a source of "anti-synergies"):
- Talent & Leadership: Retention of key people, skill complementarity.
- Culture & Change Management: Alignment in values, decision-making, employee morale.
- Integration Complexity: Systems compatibility, regulatory hurdles, geographic/cultural distances.

**Risk & Realization Factors** (cross-cutting):
- Probability & Timeline: Ease of capture (quick wins vs. long-term), required investments.
- Regulatory & External: Antitrust risks, market conditions.
- Overall Size of Opportunity: Potential $ impact relative to deal size.

## 2. Scoring Engine Design (1–5 Scale)

**Scoring Guidelines** (apply consistently with evidence/rubrics):
- **5 (Strongest Fit):** Exceptional alignment; high-confidence, material value creation (e.g., >10–20% cost savings benchmark); easy realization with low risk.
- **4 (Strong):** Good complementarity; solid upside with manageable effort.
- **3 (Moderate/Neutral):** Some benefits but limited or offset by challenges.
- **2 (Weak):** Minimal upside; notable risks or integration hurdles.
- **1 (Poor):** Negative impact likely (e.g., high cannibalization, culture clash, regulatory blocks).

**How the Engine Works:**

Data Inputs:
- Due diligence outputs (financials, org charts, customer data, tech audits).
- Benchmarks from comparable deals.
- Bottom-up estimates (e.g., function-by-function "as-is vs. to-be" analysis).
- Expert interviews or clean team assessments.

Scoring Process:
- Assign individual scores per sub-dimension (with rationale/notes).
- Optional: Score sub-factors like "Magnitude of Value" and "Probability of Realization" separately (e.g., using a matrix: High/High = premium justification).
- Apply weights based on deal context (e.g., cost synergies weighted higher in horizontal mergers; strategic higher in tech deals). Total weights = 100%.
- **Example weights:** Cost/Operational 35%, Revenue 25%, Strategic 20%, Financial 10%, Organizational 10%.

Calculation:
- **Weighted Average Score = Σ (Dimension Score × Weight).**

Overall Score Interpretation:
- **4.5–5.0:** Excellent synergy potential; strong deal rationale.
- **3.5–4.4:** Solid; pursue with focused integration plan.
- **2.5–3.4:** Marginal; requires deep scrutiny or lower premium.
- **Below 2.5:** High risk; reconsider or walk away.

Adjust for realization haircuts (e.g., model 70–85% for cost, 25–35% for revenue) and phase-in over 1–3+ years.
